#### Idea:
- **Second gripper stays on UR10e:** This need to be figured out how it works because we don't know how yet. From Google:
	The Unitree Dex1-1 gripper can be mounted on a Universal Robots UR10e robot==, though it requires a custom mechanical adapter plate and custom software integration. [[1](https://www.graspmonkey.de/en/blogs/news/iso-9409-1-die-schnittstelle-zwischen-roboter-und-werkzeug), [2](https://forum.universal-robots.com/t/connect-a-gripper-to-the-robot/21716), [3](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service)]
	
	Physical and Electrical Compatibility
	
	- **Mechanical Flange:** The UR10e features a standard ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6 tool flange (50 mm pitch circle diameter with 4x M6 taps). The Unitree Dex1-1 is natively designed for Unitree's humanoid platforms (such as the G1, H2, and R1) rather than an industrial ISO flange, meaning you will need to fabricate or source a custom adapter bracket. [[1](https://www.unitree.com/mobile/Dex1-1), [2](https://www.graspmonkey.de/en/blogs/news/iso-9409-1-die-schnittstelle-zwischen-roboter-und-werkzeug)]
	
	- **Payload & Weight:** The UR10e supports a payload of up to 12.5 kg. Weighing only 550 g and capable of handling form-fitting loads up to 20 kg or friction loads up to 5 kg, the Dex1-1 falls well within the UR10e's physical weight and force limits. [[1](https://www.universal-robots.com/media/1811481/ur10e-product-factsheet-de-web.pdf), [2](https://www.unitree.com/mobile/Dex1-1)]
	
	- **Power Supply:** The UR10e tool I/O port provides selectable 12V or 24V DC power. The Dex1-1operates across 24V–64V (with 24V or 48V DC recommended), so you can route power through the UR10e tool connector or use an external power supply matching the required voltage. [[1](https://www.usrobotstore.com/products/dex1-1-gripper-compatible-with-all-unitree-g1-models-and-h2), [2](https://forum.universal-robots.com/t/connect-a-gripper-to-the-robot/21716)]
	
	Control and Software Integration
	
	- **Communication Protocol:** The Dex1-1 communicates via a high-speed RS-485 protocol (6 Mbps baud rate, 1000 Hz control frequency) or Unitree's custom serial-to-DDS service, rather than standard industrial fieldbuses (like PROFINET, Ethernet/IP, or Modbus RTU commonly used with UR cobots).
	
	- **Control Interface:** To command the gripper from the UR10e teach pendant or a host PC, you will need a bridge computer (such as a companion Raspberry Pi, mini-PC, or ROS/ROS 2 node) running Unitree's SDK or the `dex1_1_service` to translate commands sent from the robot. [[1](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service), [2](https://www.usrobotstore.com/products/dex1-1-gripper-compatible-with-all-unitree-g1-models-and-h2)]
	- **Check (Claude, 2026-11-05)** against the [UR10e datasheet](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/latest/en/datasheets/ur10e/) and [[2026-11-03 Dex1-1 technical note]]:
		- Flange EN ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6 and payload 12.5 kg: confirmed by the datasheet. A custom flange-to-Dex1-1 adapter plate is needed: correct (the Dex1-1 has no ISO flange; its rear mount is 4 × M3, `msc-thesis/hardware/PRINT-TEST-QUEUE.md` §3).

So the tool connector is very likely **not enough** at peak; plan an external 24/48 V supply for the robot-side gripper (unverified until the peak is measured; stays in [[Waiting]]).
		- Communication: the tool connector can be set to 1 × RS-485 (datasheet), but no baud rate is stated, and the Dex1-1 runs at 6 Mbit/s (product page). The simpler route is Unitree's own: RS-485-to-USB board to a PC running `dex1_1_service`, cable routed along the arm. "1000 Hz" is the product-page figure; the service actually runs at 500 Hz (technical note).

#### Gripper signal
- Could you please explain in simple and easy to understand what these means:
	- Command + width + tau_est
	- rad → width curve
	- **Answer (Claude):**
		- **Command:** what the PC tells the gripper to do, e.g. "go to position X with stiffness kp". This is what the operator asked for with the trigger.
		- **Width (achieved position `q`):** what the gripper reports back it actually reached, 500 times per second. It differs from the command when the fingers hit an object: the command says "close", the fingers stop at the object. With the real gripper both are recorded for free; the printed copy only gives the achieved width (and only through ArUco or the AS5600).
		- **`tau_est`:** the motor's own estimate of the torque it applies, computed from motor current. It is a rough stand-in for grip force; there is no force sensor. To turn it into newtons it must be calibrated once against a force gauge.
		- **rad → width curve:** the gripper reports its position as a motor angle in radians, not as a finger gap in metres (our convention). The curve converts one into the other. Sources disagree on the scale (5.4 rad or 5.62 rad for the full stroke; 82 mm or 90 mm stroke), so it is measured: command several positions, measure the gap with calipers, fit a line. For scale: 5.62 rad × 8 mm pinion pitch radius × 2 fingers ≈ 90 mm (computed; assumes `q` is the pinion angle, unverified).
