# Dex1-1 on the UR10e: integration deep research

Question: how can a Unitree Dex1-1 be integrated on a UR10e (e-series), covering the flange adapter, power and cable routing, the RS-485 link (Unitree RS485-USB adapter vs UR Tool Communication Interface), software (`dex1_1_service`, DDS, ROS 2, `ur_rtde`), control rates and payload/TCP setup, and what problems have others reported? Builds on [[2026-11-03 Dex1-1 technical note]] (gripper-side source reading) and [[2026-11-12 LieSpline-DP review for Dex1-1 on the UR10e]] (only published UR + Dex1 precedent). **All web sources below are not in bib.**

**Method and limits.** Sources: UR10e datasheet and user manual SW 5.26 (text extraction; printed page numbers), UR support articles, URScript manual; source code read at pinned commits: `dex1_1_service` `ced3216`, `xr_teleoperate` `817fb00`, `unitree_sdk2` `aaf69cb`, `unitree_ros2` `668d1ec`, `Universal_Robots_ROS2_Driver` `f641821`, `Universal_Robots_Client_Library` `3ead504`, `ur_rtde` `e9aad76`, Orboh repos (pinned below); arXiv full text; GitHub code and issue search (English, Chinese). Blocked or not read: Unitree support page (JS app; text taken from a third-party mirror; the §4.2 wiring image was not read in this pass, but its content is in [[2026-11-03 Dex1-1 technical note]] from Martyn's download); `dex1_1_service` issues #6 and #7 (errors/rate limits) and the issues' open/closed state; UR manual drawings (flange drawing §6.3, payload curve §9.1 are images); official `ur_rtde` API page; Bilibili/Zhihu/YouTube in-site search; Printables/Thingiverse/GrabCAD. Nothing was tested on hardware. Links point to `main`/`master`; line numbers refer to the pinned commits.

## Short answer

1. **The UR tool connector can carry neither the gripper's power nor its data link.** Tool power is 12/24 V, 2 A dual-pin (24 V mode 23.5–24.8 V); the Dex1-1 wants 24–64 V and up to 3.5 A. The UR tool RS-485 accepts at most 5 Mbit/s, and the Dex1-1's M4010 motor runs at a fixed 6 Mbit/s. Use a **separate 24 or 48 V DC supply** and **Unitree's RS485-to-USB adapter to the PC**, with one cable run along the arm.
2. **The software needs no Unitree robot.** `dex1_1_service` is a serial-to-DDS bridge that runs on x86-64 Ubuntu 22.04. The arm runs on its own driver (`ur_rtde` or the UR ROS 2 driver, 500 Hz), and the gripper is a second DDS client in the same policy process. No UR or ROS 2 driver for the Dex1-1 exists.
3. **No public UR-to-Dex1-1 adapter exists.** The UR side is ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6. The Dex1-1 base pattern is not published in any text source, so it must be measured from the official STEP (already in Fusion) or from the part.
4. **Use measured mass, not URDF mass, for the payload.** URDF masses for the Dex1-1 range from 0.19 to 0.57 kg, while the spec is 0.55 kg and an independent scale measurement gave 0.546 kg. Payload and TCP are set in PolyScope or via `ur_rtde`.
5. **Reported problems are all software, calibration and model problems on Unitree robots.** They are: serial permissions, motors not replying, per-unit calibration offsets and direction, brake after a 1 s command gap, and URDF mass and stroke errors. **No one has publicly documented a Dex1-1 on a UR arm at hardware level.** LieSpline-DP uses a UR5e + "Dex1" but publishes no hardware-side program.

The 3.5 A figure is ambiguous. Unitree's spec text (mirror) calls it "peak phase current" ([mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maxtlee/HumanoidSeatAssembly/HEAD/docs/unitree/dex1-1_gripper/about_dex1_1.md)), and RoboStore calls it "peak line current" ([RoboStore](https://robostore.com/products/unitree-dex1-1-v1-standard-gripper)). If it is a phase current, the supply current is lower, but this is unverified. The UR control-box 24 V I/O supply (2 A, 3.5 A for 500 ms at 33 % duty, [manual §8.5, p. 74](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)) has the same marginal limits and is not recommended (inference).

The data path rules out the TCI even below 5 Mbit/s. On PolyScope 5 the TCI reaches the PC only through the `rs485` URCap: the robot exposes the tool device on TCP port 54321, and a `socat` script creates a virtual tty such as `/tmp/ttyUR` ([`setup_tool_communication.rst` L8-90](https://github.com/UniversalRobots/Universal_Robots_ROS2_Driver/blob/main/ur_robot_driver/doc/setup_tool_communication.rst)). `dex1_1_service` runs a 500 Hz request/response serial loop, so a TCP-tunnelled tty is unsuitable (inference, unmeasured). No source shows that the M4010 baud rate can be changed; it sits inside the closed `libUnitreeMotorSDK_Linux64.so`. `ur_rtde` has no tool-communication API at all (local grep of [ur_rtde](https://gitlab.com/sdurobotics/ur_rtde)).

On emergency stop the UR tool power pins go to 0 V ([manual §9.3, p. 103](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). An external supply does **not** inherit this. Whether the gripper's supply should be switched by the UR safety I/O is a design decision with no precedent found.

## Wiring: separate supply and RS485-USB to the PC

The Dex1-1 kit includes a "485-to-USB conversion board" and cables ([RoboStore](https://robostore.com/products/unitree-dex1-1-v1-standard-gripper)). Power and RS-485 share one XT30PW(2+2)-M connector on the gripper ([support-page mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maxtlee/HumanoidSeatAssembly/HEAD/docs/unitree/dex1-1_gripper/about_dex1_1.md)). In Unitree's connection diagram (read from Martyn's download, [[2026-11-03 Dex1-1 technical note]]), the topology is PC → USB → adapter → RS-485 → gripper. The DC supply joins the cable between the adapter and the gripper, and the adapter has four UART ports (G, A, B). Unitree confirms that use with "your own PC and robotic arm" is "theoretically feasible" by this diagram ([`dex1_1_service` #5](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/issues/5)).

The UR manual gives only general cable rules. Cables should be coiled and held ([§5.2, p. 39](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). Shielded cable is required for I/O ([§8, pp. 93, 95](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). Applications where the controller I/O cables flex are not allowed ([§8.1, p. 67](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). No UR dress-pack guidance was found. A run that carries 6 Mbit/s RS-485 and power along the arm should therefore be treated as design judgement (unverified): use a shielded twisted pair with the power pair in one high-flex cable, strain relief at the adapter plate, a service loop at wrist 3, and wrist-3 joint limits in the safety configuration against wind-up. The supplied cable's length is not stated, so it is unknown whether it reaches from the flange to a PC beside the robot. The USB leg (adapter at the base or on the PC) is the obvious place to add length (inference).

The tool flange is at GND ([manual §9.3, p. 101](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). A metal adapter therefore bonds the Dex1-1 housing to UR ground. Whether the housing is tied to the supply negative is unknown, so check continuity before the first power-up (inference).

## Flange adapter: UR side known, Dex1-1 side must be measured

The UR10e flange is **EN ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6**, with an M8 8-pin tool connector ([datasheet](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/latest/en/datasheets/ur10e/)). The tool is fixed with four M6 class 8.8 bolts at 8 Nm, and a Ø6 mm dowel hole sets the repeatable position ([manual §6.3, p. 47](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)). Bolts must not extend more than 8 mm into the flange, because "very long M6 bolts can press against the bottom of the tool flange and short circuit the robot" ([UR article](https://www.universal-robots.com/articles/ur/application-installation/end-effector-mounting-on-tool-output-flange/)). By the ISO designation the bolt circle is Ø50 mm, 4 × M6 (unverified). The centring-bore diameter (often quoted as Ø31.5 H7) and the dowel tolerance and angle are not read from a UR drawing (unverified); take them from UR's flange drawing or CAD before machining.

The Dex1-1 side has no public pattern. Unitree's product page lists only "Standalone use" ([unitree.com](https://www.unitree.com/Dex1-1/)). RobotShop gives connector, mounting interface and flange as "Not specified" ([RobotShop](https://www.robotshop.com/products/unitree-dex1-1-v1-hand-for-unitree-h2-left)). The Orboh handheld repo has no CAD at all (`find` on [Dex1-1hand_UMI @ 3a6b282](https://github.com/Orboh/Dex1-1hand_UMI)). Searches for UR, Franka or other-arm adapters returned nothing ([UR Marketplace hit](https://universal-robots.com/marketplace/products/01tP40000071NgLIAU) was unrelated). **No precedent exists.** The official `Dex1_1_with_camera.STEP` is already imported in Fusion (`dex1-1-ref`, per `dex1-1-handheld/hardware/dex1-1-reference/README.md`), so the base pattern can be read from it and checked on the part.

## Software: two drivers, one policy loop

`dex1_1_service` is a small C++ bridge. It scans `/dev/ttyUSB*`, `/dev/ttyCH343USB*` and `/dev/ttyACM*` ([`main.cpp` L21-41](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L21-L41)) and probes M4010 IDs 0 (right) and 1 (left) ([L179-196](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L179-L196)). It subscribes `unitree_go::msg::dds_::MotorCmds_` on `rt/dex1/{right,left}/cmd` and publishes `MotorStates_` on `rt/dex1/{right,left}/state` ([L120-127](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L120-L127)). Commands are output-side `q` (rad), `dq`, `tau` (N·m), `kp` and `kd`, scaled by the gear ratio inside the service ([L73-85](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L73-L85)). The state carries only `q`, `dq` and `tau_est`; temperature and error codes are dropped ([L82-87](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L82-L87)). The `mode` field is ignored and the service always runs FOC ([L70-79](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L70-L79)). If no command arrives for **1 s**, the motor goes to BRAKE with all gains zero ([`Subscription.h` L31-51](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/include/dds/Subscription.h#L31-L51); [`main.cpp` L65-69](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/main.cpp#L65-L69)). The service builds against `unitree_sdk2` and CycloneDDS C++, and ships amd64 `libserialport` debs for Ubuntu 22.04 ([CMakeLists L24-44](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/CMakeLists.txt#L24-L44); [README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/README.md)). It runs with `sudo`, uses DDS domain 0, and selects the interface with `-n` (default `eth0`, [`param.h` L28-33](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/main/include/param.h#L28-L33)). The README warns that `eth0` can be wrong and that the autostart script hard-codes it (FAQ 3). Running on `lo` with all clients on one PC is untested (unverified).

The client side has no Dex1-specific API. Clients publish `MotorCmds_` via `unitree_sdk2` (C++) or `unitree_sdk2py`, as in Unitree's `Dex1_1_Gripper_Controller` ([`robot_hand_unitree.py` L5-8, L232-268](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/xr_teleoperate/blob/main/teleop/robot_control/robot_hand_unitree.py#L232-L268)). **No ROS 2 driver for the Dex1-1 exists.** `unitree_ros2` has the `unitree_go/MotorCmds` messages but no "dex1" code ([unitree_ros2](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_ros2/tree/master/cyclonedds_ws/src/unitree/unitree_go/msg)). The only third-party "Dex1 driver" emulates the Dex1 topics for an EZGripper ([SAKErobotics](https://github.com/SAKErobotics/unitree-dex1-ezgripper-driver)). A ROS 2 node with `rmw_cyclonedds_cpp` publishing `/dex1/right/cmd` should map to DDS topic `rt/dex1/right/cmd` through the ROS 2 `rt/` prefix (inference, untested). Orboh had to serialise CycloneDDS channel creation with a lock, because concurrent type registration raised "Failed to encode union … DDS.XTypes.TypeObject" (CycloneDDS 0.10.5; [`g1_gripper_connection.py` L122-135](https://github.com/Orboh/DimOS_base_G1-TOYOTA-BODY-RESEARCH/blob/d170b170a85245ccd906ce85174a38b4b3e5942a/dimos/robot/unitree/g1/act/g1_gripper_connection.py#L122-L135)).

For the arm, there are two documented paths. `ur_rtde` `RTDEControlInterface` defaults to 500 Hz on e-series, offers `servoL`/`servoJ` (lookahead 0.03–0.2 s, gain 100–2000) and uses `initPeriod`/`waitPeriod` for low-jitter loops; it is "not thread-safe" ([`rtde_control_interface.h` L245-262, L329-341, L512-525](https://gitlab.com/sdurobotics/ur_rtde/-/blob/master/include/ur_rtde/rtde_control_interface.h)). The UR ROS 2 driver runs ros2_control at 500 Hz for the ur10e ([`ur10e_update_rate.yaml` L3](https://github.com/UniversalRobots/Universal_Robots_ROS2_Driver/blob/main/ur_robot_driver/config/ur10e_update_rate.yaml)) and streams via `servoj` in its URScript ([`external_control.urscript` L427-450](https://github.com/UniversalRobots/Universal_Robots_Client_Library/blob/master/resources/external_control.urscript)). It has no third-party gripper controller ([`ur_controllers.yaml`](https://github.com/UniversalRobots/Universal_Robots_ROS2_Driver/blob/main/ur_robot_driver/config/ur_controllers.yaml)). Unitree's own teleop uses the same split as a UR setup would: the arm and the gripper controller run in separate processes sharing values, with the gripper at 200 Hz and the main loop at 30 Hz ([`teleop_hand_and_arm.py` L76, L200-208](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/xr_teleoperate/blob/main/teleop/teleop_hand_and_arm.py#L200-L208)). LieSpline-DP splits a policy process from a hardware "Robot Service" over ZeroMQ with heartbeat and staleness checks, but does not publish the service ([[2026-11-12 LieSpline-DP review for Dex1-1 on the UR10e]]). Arm and gripper are not hardware-synchronised. Both states must be stamped on the PC clock at receipt (project convention).

## Payload and TCP: measure the mass

The UR10e datasheet gives 12.5 kg ([datasheet](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/latest/en/datasheets/ur10e/)). Manual §3.1 lists "10 kg or 12.5 kg" ([p. 18](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW5_26/user-manual-UR10e-PDF_online/711-039-00_UR10e_User_Manual_en_Global.pdf)); which applies to the lab unit is unverified, though either rating leaves wide margin at under 1 kg. The rated payload depends on the CoG offset (curve in §9.1, image only). Mass and CoG are set in the installation, or at runtime with `set_target_payload()`, which replaces the deprecated `set_payload` ([UR article](https://www.universal-robots.com/articles/ur/programming/how-to-change-payload-and-center-of-gravity-settings-using-urscript/)). PolyScope's Payload Estimation uses four taught poses that differ as much as possible ([PolyScope X manual](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW10_15/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR5e/PolyX_SW/psx_payload_set.htm)). `ur_rtde` exposes `setPayload`, `setTargetPayload`, `setTcp` and `getTCPOffset` on the control side, and `getPayload`, `getPayloadCog` and `getPayloadInertia` on the receive side ([API reference mirror](https://xilab-robotics.github.io/Physics-Informed-Neural-Networks/_downloads/0bdb8e040794fc48c93c028b97b750c2/ur_rtde_api_reference_example.pdf)); exact signatures are unverified because the official page did not load.

The model masses disagree. The URDF in `dex1_1_service` sums to about 0.19 kg ([`dex1_1.urdf`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dex1_1_service/blob/ced3216290bb22dae356213993786beeaa7143ab/urdf/dex1_1.urdf)). The G1 + Dex1-1 URDF gives 0.365 kg, against **0.546 kg measured** on a kitchen scale. That gap caused gravity-compensation sag on a G1, which was fixed by scaling to 0.55 kg ([Orboh #30](https://github.com/Orboh/DimOS_base_G1-TOYOTA-BODY-RESEARCH/issues/30)). The official URDF `Dex1-1_URDF_261030` sums to 0.571 kg including the camera (`dex1-1-handheld/hardware/dex1-1-reference/README.md`). The payload should therefore be the weighed gripper + camera + adapter + cable clamp, with the CoG from CAD or estimation. The TCP should be set at the fingertip centre; the official model puts the fingertips at x = 0.1433 m from the base (same README), plus adapter thickness. Version the TCP with the calibration files.

No reports were found of overheating, wear or grip-force shortfall, and none from a non-Unitree arm. The user base is small and Chinese forums were not searchable, so this absence is weak evidence. The closest relative of this thesis is Orboh's Dex1-1 UMI handheld, but it uses the Dex1-1 unpowered, as a cutter: it records no gripper width, and its policy cannot command the gripper ([`umi_dex1.yaml` L1-4](https://github.com/Orboh/Dex1-1hand_UMI/blob/3a6b282e16ffb6378aebe5d8af9810bbe869ba78/diffusion_policy/config/task/umi_dex1.yaml); [`umi_diffusion_bridge.py` L17-39](https://github.com/Orboh/DimOS_base_G1-TOYOTA-BODY-RESEARCH/blob/d170b170a85245ccd906ce85174a38b4b3e5942a/dimos/robot/unitree/g1/act/umi_diffusion_bridge.py#L17-L39)). Its deployment target is a G1, not a UR. Correction to one research note: LieSpline-DP does have a project page and an MIT code repo (commit `6ec4677`); only the hardware-side program is unpublished ([[2026-11-12 LieSpline-DP review for Dex1-1 on the UR10e]]).

## Existing UR10e station (Martyn, 2026-11-12)

Martyn's description of the current station: a Robotiq 2F-85 and an Intel RealSense D435i are on the tool flange. The 2F-85 uses RS-485. Its cable goes to a power supply under the station, and its data lines go through an RS-485-to-USB converter into a Raspberry Pi. The D435i is also connected to the Pi by USB. The setup was used to train a π0.5 policy on VR-teleop data.

This has four consequences (all inference, not checked on the station):
- The station already uses the topology this note recommends for the Dex1-1: an external supply, plus RS-485 to USB to a host computer, outside the UR tool connector. The existing cable route along the arm and the mounting point for the supply can probably be reused.
- The existing supply and cable may not fit the Dex1-1 as they are. The Dex1-1 needs 24–64 V at up to 3.5 A peak and RS-485 at 6 Mbit/s. The station's supply rating and the cable's type and length are not known here, so check both before reuse.
- The Dex1-1 adapter replaces the 2F-85 mount, so it must also hold the D435i or keep its current mount clear.
- Which computer runs `dex1_1_service` (the Pi or the lab PC) is decided separately in [[2026-11-12 Raspberry Pi vs lab PC for the Dex1-1]].

## Integration checklist (to measure or decide)

- [ ] Dex1-1 base bolt pattern, thread size and pilot: read from the official STEP in Fusion, then check on the delivered part.
- [ ] UR flange centring bore, dowel position and tolerance: take from UR's flange drawing or CAD; choose screw lengths so engagement stays ≤ 8 mm.
- [ ] Adapter: material (metal bonds the housing to UR GND) and camera clearance; check continuity between housing and supply negative.
- [ ] Existing station supply (under the UR10e) and 2F-85 cable route: read the rating and cable type; reuse if they meet 24–64 V / 3.5 A and 6 Mbit/s RS-485.
- [ ] Supply: 24 V or 48 V, rated ≥ 3.5 A plus margin; measure actual peak supply current at max grip. Decide whether the UR safety I/O or e-stop switches it.
- [ ] Cable: length of the supplied cable; extension on the power/RS-485 leg or the USB leg; strain relief, service loop and wrist-3 limits.
- [ ] Gripper motor ID (0 = `right`) and `/dev/ttyUSB*` udev rule (no `chmod 777`).
- [ ] `dex1_1_service` on x86-64: `-n` interface (`lo` vs a real NIC); measured state rate against the nominal 500 Hz.
- [ ] Calibration (closed = 0) per unit; record the open-end q; check direction; version the result.
- [ ] Map q to width in metres with calipers over the full stroke, to settle the 82 vs 90 mm discrepancy.
- [ ] Command stream: continuous at ≥ the policy rate, well above 1 Hz; behaviour on policy stop (brake after 1 s).
- [ ] Arm driver: `ur_rtde` `servoL` or the ROS 2 driver; one process or two; PC-clock stamps for both states.
- [ ] Payload: weighed mass (gripper + camera + adapter + clamp), CoG from CAD or Payload Estimation; TCP at the fingertip centre.
