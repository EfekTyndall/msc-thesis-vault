How the lab computers, robot and grippers connect

Today a small Raspberry Pi at the robot station passes gripper and camera data to a far-away computer. The handheld can use the same idea, with one change: for the Dex1-1, the Pi has to run Unitree's gripper program itself instead of only passing messages on.

Pi at the stationA messenger. It sits there because USB and gripper cables can't reach the far computers.
Jetson and fractal-pcBoth far away. Either one can run XRoboToolkit for the PICO headset.

Why the Dex1-1 can't simply be forwarded

Think of the Robotiq as letters and the Dex1-1 as a phone call with no pauses. The Robotiq gets a short message and answers it, up to about 200 times a second, and a few milliseconds of travel don't matter. Unitree's program talks to the Dex1-1 motor every 2 ms and waits for the answer each time. If the Pi only forwarded these messages, every exchange would go across the network and back, and it would have to fit inside those 2 ms.

Illustration only: the round-trip time in this lab has not been measured, and real networks also have occasional spikes. The 2 ms loop and the brake when commands stop come from Unitree's code (dex1_1_service). The bar is the time one exchange takes; the black line is the 2 ms the Dex1-1 motor allows. The Robotiq has no such limit: a slower network only means fewer updates per second (its usual rate is about 200/s).

fractal-pc or the Jetson?

Both stand far from the station, and both can run XRoboToolkit. For the handheld they would do exactly the same job, so the choice comes down to practical reasons.

Ideas looked at and dropped

IdeaWhy not
Plug the handheld straight into fractal-pcThe PC is too far from the station for USB and gripper cables.
Forward the Dex1-1 through the Pi like the RobotiqThe 2 ms motor loop can't cross the network (see above).
Power and drive the Dex1-1 through the UR10e wrist connectorThe wrist gives 12/24 V at 2 A and RS-485 up to 5 Mbit/s. The Dex1-1 needs 24–64 V, 3.5 A peak and 6 Mbit/s.
A Pi replaces fractal-pc entirelyNo hardware video encoder, no GPU for training, and XRoboToolkit is untested on a Pi.
A carried computer (Jetson in a backpack)Possible on paper, but out of the thesis scope (dropped 12 Nov).
