From Recording to Robot

How a handheld recording becomes robot motion in the Replay Ladder: four steps, what each one calculates, and why the first three matter even when no robot is in the room.

Numbers are from take pose-log-20261125-150606/take-01 (30.4 s), replayed 2026-11-25 on the Mac and on the real UR10e (RL4 (Robotiq open/close during replay)). Papers in the thesis bibliography are cited by key; anything else is marked not in bib. Code: dex1-1-handheld/software/sim/ur10e_replay.py (steps 1–3) and ur10e_replay_rtde.py (step 4).

The short version

A recording only says where the operator's hand went and how far the trigger was pressed. A robot needs something else: six joint angles every 8 ms, plus "open" and "close" at the right moments. It also needs to know in advance that it can reach every point safely. The pipeline translates the first into the second and checks it before anything moves.

Martyn's reading is right. During handheld collection there is no robot, so nobody sees whether a demonstration is actually possible for the UR10e. Steps 1–3 run on a laptop without the robot. They act as that check: a take that the robot could not follow, or that comes too close to the table, is flagged right after recording. Step 4 is only needed to prove, once, that what the check predicts is what the real robot does. That proof is the Replay Ladder.

Step 1

Read the recording

Input: raw.csv from the recorder. Each row holds the PC time, the controller position (x, y, z) and orientation (a quaternion, four numbers for a rotation), and the trigger value 0–1. Take-01 has 2187 rows in 30.4 s (about 72 rows per second, from a 90 Hz stream).

Calculated:

  • Time of each row in seconds from the first row. The replay keeps the recorded timing.
  • Where the fingertip is, not the controller. The tracked point sits inside the controller, so a fixed offset from the Fusion assembly (hand_tip in the settings file) moves it to the midpoint between the Dex1-1 dummy's finger pads.
  • Movement since the first row, in the fingertip's own frame: "5 cm forward along the fingers, turned 10° about the jaw axis". The operator's absolute position in the room is not used.

Why: the robot cannot copy absolute room positions, because the handheld's room is not the robot's room. UMI-style systems use the same relative movement (chi2024). This is the action format chosen in D07 (software and data format).

Step 2

Rehearse in simulation

Calculated, every 8 ms (125 times per second, the robot's own rate):

  • Target for the robot fingertip. The start pose HOME_FACE plus the movement from step 1. The robot fingertip is the midpoint between the Robotiq pads, 154 mm beyond the flange.
  • Joint angles (inverse kinematics, IK). The team's UR10e solver finds the six joint angles that put the fingertip on the target. Take-01: the solution was at most 3.3 mm from the target, and the solver never gave up (0 of 3921 solves).
  • Speed limit. No joint may turn faster than 0.3 rad/s, as in the team's VR teleop on the real UR10e. If a hand movement was faster, the step is shortened and the arm lags behind. Take-01: the limit was active for 0.7 s, highest joint speed 0.25 rad/s.
  • Simulated arm (MuJoCo). A model of the UR10e with a Robotiq 2F-85 actually moves to those angles. Each next solve starts from where the simulated arm really is. Take-01: the simulated fingertip stayed within 18.6 mm of the target (RMS 5.7 mm).
  • Gripper commands. The trigger values become a short list: close when the trigger reaches 0.9, open again only when it drops below 0.8 (6 commands in take-01). The same gripper program as on the real robot sends them to the simulated gripper.

Why simulate, and not just compute angles? Three reasons:

  • The solver needs a "current arm pose" to start from. Starting each solve from the previous answer made the team solver fail often (seen 2026-11-23). A simulated arm gives a realistic current pose without a robot.
  • It shows the path the arm actually takes with the speed limit, not only the wished-for path. The checks in step 3 use that path.
  • One IK solve takes about 20 ms on fractal-pc, too slow for the 8 ms robot loop. Computing everything beforehand lets step 4 just send numbers.

Not URSim. The simulation is MuJoCo inside the same Python program. URSim (UR's own controller simulator) was used only in RL2 (replay against URSim), to test the network commands.

If the floor or area check fails, the program stops before connecting to the robot. Example: on 2026-11-23 take-01 of the desk recording was refused at 242 mm (allowed 150 mm).

Not checked yet: collisions with objects on the table or with the robot itself, and joint torques. The MuJoCo model has collision shapes, but the replay does not report contacts. These are open for when objects come into the scene (RL6 (swap to the Dex1-1)).

Step 4

Play it on the real robot

What happens: the program connects to the UR controller (RTDE, UR's network interface) and moves the arm to HOME_FACE. Then it sends the precomputed joint angles every 8 ms with servoJ (UR's "go to these angles now" command, team settings). A second loop checks 20 times a second whether a gripper command is due and sends it to the Robotiq through the station Pi. Both start together. Nothing is computed live, except reading back where the robot actually is.

Logged: commanded and measured joint angles, target and actual fingertip, gripper commands and measured opening. These logs show whether steps 1–3 predicted the robot correctly.

Why the real robot at all? To show that the laptop check can be trusted. The simulation and the robot agree to within a few millimetres and tens of milliseconds, so in the thesis collection steps 1–3 can stand in for the robot.

Words used on this page

IK (inverse kinematics)
Finding the joint angles that put the gripper at a wanted position and angle.
MuJoCo
Physics simulator; runs a model of the UR10e and Robotiq on the laptop.
servoJ
UR command: move to these six joint angles within the next 8 ms.
HOME_FACE
Start pose saved on the pendant: gripper level, facing the operator.
Hysteresis
Two thresholds (close at 0.9, open below 0.8) so a trigger resting near one value cannot toggle the gripper.
Replay Ladder
Steps RL1–RL6 that replay recordings on ever more real setups, from Mac simulation to the Dex1-1 on the UR10e.
