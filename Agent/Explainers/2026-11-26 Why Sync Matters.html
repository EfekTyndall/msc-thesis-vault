Replay Ladder · RL5 (camera and recorder)

Why sync matters

Every camera image has to be stored with the hand position from the same moment. If the position comes from a little later, the training data shows the policy the gripper in the wrong place. This page shows how big that error is, what the RL5 test takes found, and how the delay will be measured.

1 / 3

A late pose is a wrong pose

The camera and the PICO controller each arrive at the recording computer with their own delay. If the recorder pairs an image with the pose that arrives at the same moment, that pose was taken later than the image. The hand has moved on in between.

Gap = hand speed × delay. At 0.3 m/s (assumed, unverified), a 50 ms delay puts the stored pose 15 mm away from where the camera saw the gripper.

What the RL5 test takes showed

Two problems came up on 26 Nov. Both are drawn here from the real take files (every frame), not from summary numbers.

Problem 1: the station Pi's clock is 16.44 s behind, and drifting

D435i on the station Pi, 60 s take, 1800 frames (r4-pi/take-01.mcap). Each dot: arrival time on the Jetson minus the Pi's stamp, minus 16 440 ms. The line rises 0.065 ms per second (65 ppm, about 4 ms per minute). Fixed by moving the camera to the station Jetson: one clock, arrival − stamp stayed at 32.5 ms with no drift (R4b).

Problem 2: the old recorder lost 2.5 s of video

Time from each frame to the next, per take, D435i on the Jetson; red bars = time with no frames. Normal is 33.4 ms (30 fps). The old recorder read the camera and wrote the file in one loop; one write blocked it, and about 74 frames were lost at 26 s. Fix: the file is written by its own thread. Three 60 s takes since: no hole mid-take. The 0.4 s gap at the start of each is the camera opening, now skipped (fix desk-tested only). A 10 min take is still to do.

How the delay will be measured: the board wave

Tape the controller to a printed marker board and wave it in front of the camera. The camera sees the board move; the controller reports its own movement. Both draw the same wave, one slightly late. Slide one curve until the two lie on top of each other: the slide is the delay.

shift 0 ms · mismatch – mm

Illustration with invented curves (true delay set to 42 ms). The real numbers come from the RL5 sync run. Same matching idea as UMI's gripper check (chi2024, App. A) and the supervisor's analyze_diag.py (not read for this page). FastUMI (zhaxizhuoma2025) skips measuring and pairs nearest samples on one clock.

  1. Record 30 s of waving: camera images and controller poses with their arrival times.
  2. Find the board's position in each image.
  3. Shift until the curves match; store that delay as a versioned file. Every later take is lined up with it.
